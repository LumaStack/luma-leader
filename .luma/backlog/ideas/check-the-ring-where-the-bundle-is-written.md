# Check a bundle's ring where the bundle is written

**A bundle's ring is what a reader actually sees, and nobody upstream ever sees
it.** It is generated by `luma-foreman apply`, in the adopting project, from a
bundle written somewhere else entirely. The author who wrote the bundle and the
curator who published it have both never laid eyes on the artifact their work
turns into.

**So the check belongs upstream too** — in `luma-catalog-curator` when a catalog
is curated, and in `bundle-manager`'s `audit-bundle` when a bundle is worked on.

## What went wrong, which is how this surfaced

`lumastack/luma-catalog/git-secrets` holds five Documents: two policies and three
workflows. **Its ring names two of them and says nothing about the other three.**
A reader opening that ring concludes the bundle contains two things.

The omission is deliberate and correct — workflows reach this harness as skills,
and an adapter must not render what its harness already carries. **The silence
about it is the defect.** The line explaining that lives in the *project* ring,
which may be far out of context, or never read at all by somebody who arrived
through `/load-bundle`.

**Nothing caught it.** Not `apply`, not `inspect` — the reachability check passes
because the workflows genuinely are reachable, as skills. They are simply
invisible from the artifact that claims to say what the bundle holds. It was
found by a person reading the output as a reader rather than as its author,
which is not a repeatable process.

## What "checked" means, and it is not byte comparison

The curator cannot render the same bytes foreman does and should not try — see
below. It can check **properties a good ring has**:

- **Every Document accounted for.** Named, or explicitly explained as arriving by
  another route. A count that does not add up is the defect above.
- **Descriptions present and distinguishable.** A ring is descriptions; two
  Documents whose lines read alike make the ring useless at its one job.
- **Nothing said twice.** The `entrypoint` line currently repeats a list entry
  verbatim in every bundle that declares one.
- **Readable standing alone.** A ring is reached directly, so it may not assume
  anything the project ring said is still in context.

## The trap: this would be the second consumer of ring logic

`luma-foreman` renders rings. A curator that rendered them too would be the
second implementation of the same domain logic, and `DECISIONS.md` has a settled
position — *no shared package until two real consumers exist* — with a four-part
re-open trigger. **Two of the four are met:**

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Two real tools exist, not one and a plan | **met** |
| They duplicate *domain* logic, not utilities | **met, if the curator renders** |
| The duplication has already bitten, with copies drifting into a real bug | not met |
| The shape has stopped moving | **not met** — the ring changed three times in a day |

**So do not extract, and do not render.** Check properties instead. That keeps
one renderer, and it is the better check anyway: what matters is whether the
ring represents the bundle honestly, not whether two programs agree byte for
byte.

## The two homes, doing different halves

**`luma-catalog-curator`** — mechanical, catalog-wide, runs before publication.
Every bundle in the catalog, every time. This is where a missing description or
an unaccounted Document should fail.

**`bundle-manager`'s `audit-bundle`** — the authoring half, and prose. An author
should be told to look at what their bundle will *become*, because the ring is
the thing a reader meets and the bundle directory is not. That workflow already
checks for defects that are silent; this is another one.

## Notes

**`luma-foreman inspect` should probably check it too**, at the other end, where
the ring actually exists. That is a third place and the cheapest of the three,
but it is also the last to find out — the bundle is already published and
adopted by then.

**The general shape is worth naming.** Anything generated in one repository from
material authored in another has this gap: the author cannot see the artifact.
Skills have it as well — `apply` writes a `SKILL.md` per workflow and no bundle
author ever sees one.
