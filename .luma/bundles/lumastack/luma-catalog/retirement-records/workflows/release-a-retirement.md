# Release a retirement

**The instinct is to release on a count, and the count is the wrong measure.**
Zero hits means nobody wrote it lately, not that nobody will write it again.

## 1. Apply the test, which is about the referent

**An entry may be released when nothing in the product still needs a name for
what the idea named.** That is answerable by looking rather than by counting, and
it is the only test that holds.

Worked, on this estate's own entries:

| idea | named | releasable |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `refit` | a command that was **removed** rather than renamed | **yes** — nothing needs a word for it |
| `outfit` | the command now called `apply` — still there | **no** |
| `projection` | what `apply` writes — still there | **no** |

**`refit` is the worked example.** Its count reached zero *and* the thing it named
ceased to exist. The other two never reach that state while `apply` does.

## 2. The second condition, rarely reached

**The decision that retired it was archived, with nothing superseding it.** A
retirement nothing decides is not in force, and the strategy should go with it.

## 3. Do not release on time passing

Not after a number of releases, not after a number of sessions, not because the
count has been zero for a while. **No duration has been observed to make
reinvention less likely** — the mechanism is that the retired word is the obvious
one, and obviousness does not decay.

*If evidence ever shows otherwise — a word retired long ago and genuinely
unreinventable by somebody arriving cold — that is worth recording, and it would
re-open this.*

## 4. Archive rather than delete

`lifecycle_status: archived`. **The record stays.** Somebody will ask why the word
is absent, and *"we decided against it in 2026"* is a better answer than silence
— which is how a retired idea returns as a fresh proposal.

Bump the bundle, regenerate [[what-we-retired]], and say in the `## Version` entry
which test it passed.
